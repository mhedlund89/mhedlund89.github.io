What Torty can do

Everything below is the real app, shown with sample snippets.

Abbreviations that expand anywhere

Give a snippet an abbreviation like zsig or ;date. Type it in Mail, Slack, a browser or your code editor and it's replaced on the spot, with a soft pop so you know it worked.

  • Plain text, rich text and code snippets
  • Organize snippets into groups
  • Match case exactly, ignore it, or adapt to how you typed it

Search from anywhere

Press Control Option Space in any app to search every snippet and insert one without remembering its abbreviation. Your recent snippets are one keystroke away.

  • Create a snippet straight from your clipboard
  • Jump back to edit the last snippet you used

Fill-ins, dates and smart pieces

Snippets can ask you for a name before they expand, drop in today's date, paste your clipboard, include another snippet, or park the cursor right where you'll keep typing.

Torty also notices phrases you type over and over and suggests turning them into snippets. That stays on your Mac, and you can turn it off.

And a turtle

Click Torty on the home screen for a joke, a word of the day or a turtle fact. Click the little shell at the bottom of the sidebar to come back home. It rocks.

Snippet codes

Put these inside any snippet. A plain % always stays a %, so AMPscript, Liquid and CSS come through exactly as written.

[[cursor]]Leave the cursor here after expanding
[[clipboard]]Whatever text is on your clipboard
[[date]] Today's date, like 2026-10-30
[[date:%B %e, %Y]]Any date format, like October 30, 2026
[[time]]The current time
[[key:tab]]Press a key: tab, return, esc, arrows
[[snippet:zsig]]Include another snippet by its abbreviation
[[filltext:name=First name]]Ask for a value before expanding
[[fillarea:name=Notes]]Ask for a longer, multi-line value
[[fillpopup:name=Tone:default=Friendly:Formal]]Pick from a list of choices

Getting set up

For Macs with Apple silicon (M1 or newer) on macOS 11 or later. The full steps are in the README that comes with the app.

  1. Install

    Unzip the file and drag Torty Text into your Applications folder.

  2. Open it once

    macOS will block it the first time. Go to System Settings, then Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway.

  3. Allow keyboard access

    In Privacy & Security, then Accessibility, turn on Torty Text. Quit and reopen it, and you're ready to type.

If macOS says the app "is damaged", open Terminal, run this line, then open the app again: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Torty Text.app"
